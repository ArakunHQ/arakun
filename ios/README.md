# Arakun for iOS

A native SwiftUI app for your sessions on the go: you sign in with GitHub or a code
sent by email, pick one of your account's computers and reach it through the relay the way the phone page
(`../phone/`) does, end to end encrypted.

```
ios/
  project.yml        the Xcode project, generated by xcodegen
  Arakun/            the app: sign-in, computers, inbox, a live session and what it changed, new session
  Notifications/     the notification service extension: decrypts each notice
  ArakunKit/         the protocol, testable on a Mac
    Sources/ArakunKit/E2E.swift        P-256 handshake, HKDF keys, AES-GCM frames (phone/e2e.js)
    Sources/ArakunKit/Account.swift    sign-in with GitHub, /me, /machines, /sessions (core/src/cloud.rs)
    Sources/ArakunKit/RelayLink.swift  the WebSocket to the relay, pings, {part}, {req} (phone/relay.js)
    Sources/ArakunKit/WebPush.swift    push keys, RFC 8291 decryption of notices (core/src/phone/push.rs)
    Sources/ArakunKit/Models.swift     inbox, session, entries, state, changes, diffs (core/src/phone/wire.rs)
    Sources/ArakunKit/Settings.swift   model and reasoning, new-session options, plan limits (wire.rs)
```

## Building

Xcode 16 or newer and `xcodegen` (`brew install xcodegen`):

```sh
cd ios
xcodegen generate
open Arakun.xcodeproj         # pick your team under Signing, then run
(cd ArakunKit && swift test)  # the protocol against phone/test/vectors
```

`swift test` leaves the real Keychain alone. To also check the session's round trip
through it, run `ARAKUN_KEYCHAIN_TESTS=1 swift test` on a Mac with an unlocked
keychain.

Without an account or a computer, launch with `-demo` (Debug builds only): a
signed-in sample account and sample sessions fill the app. `-demoOpen s1` opens a
session, `-demoNew YES` the New session sheet, `-demoSignIn YES` the sign-in screen,
`-demoMachines YES` the list of computers, `-demoSettings YES` (with `-demoOpen`)
the session's model and reasoning, `-demoLimits YES` the plan limits, `-demoSecret private` (or `clear`, for a
connection that is not encrypted) the secret card in the open session, and
`-demoMoods YES` every mood of the raccoon side by side:

```sh
xcrun simctl launch booted com.arakun.ios -demo -demoOpen s1
```

## Design

The app follows the brand book (`.claude/skills/arakun-brand`): black and white grounds
with no tint, amber only for the one primary button and the live dot, Geist and Geist
Mono (`assets/fonts`, which `project.yml` reads from there), the line icons from `assets/icons`, and the
raccoon. Every color, font and corner comes from `Arakun/Theme.swift`; it
follows the system's dark or light setting.

The raccoon is alive, as on the desktop: `Arakun/Mascot.swift` is a smaller port of
`core/src/mascot.rs`, drawn from the same `assets/brand/mascot.json` (read from there,
not copied). It blinks and looks around, and shows the mood of what it
watches: thinking dots, reading while the agent works, a hopping question mark when a
session needs you, a tick when a turn ends, a shake and an exclamation mark on an
error, and sleep when the computer is offline or nothing happens for two minutes. A tap
makes it hop. One raccoon per screen: the sign-in screen, the inbox header (the mood of
all sessions) and the composer of a session (that session's mood). With Reduce Motion
it holds still.

## CI and TestFlight

CI's iOS job (`.github/workflows/ci.yml`) runs `swift test` and builds the app unsigned
for the simulator when `ios/`, `phone/`, the mascot's drawing or the fonts change.
`.github/workflows/ios-release.yml` signs the app and uploads it to TestFlight: after
every release that is not a pre-release, or by hand (`gh workflow run ios-release.yml --ref main`). It needs an
App Store Connect API key, the `release` environment's one that macOS notarization uses
too, with the Admin role (App Manager can't use cloud signing, so the export fails), and skips until there is one;
the steps are at the top of that workflow.

## Signing in

Press **Sign in with GitHub**: the app shows a code, **Open GitHub** takes you to
github.com/login/device to enter it. Without GitHub, type your address and press
**Email me a code**, then type the six-digit code from the mail. It works once, for
10 minutes. Use the account you signed in with in Arakun on your computers: an
account made by email is not the same as one made with GitHub. The app then lists the account's computers; with one it
opens it straight away, and it opens the one you picked last at the next launch.
Every computer signed in to the account is there, and the relay lets in only
devices of the same account. The handshake is checked against the key the account
lists for the computer, so the relay cannot pass you to another one.

The session stays in the Keychain on this device only, never in UserDefaults or a
log. Every request that carries it, the relay's socket too, goes through one
`URLSession` that keeps no cache, cookies or credentials (`AccountNetwork` in
`Account.swift`) and follows no redirect; nothing uses `URLSession.shared`.
Settings (the More menu) lists the account's signed-in devices, each with Sign
out, and signs this phone out. A 401 from the account host returns to the sign-in
screen. iOS keeps the Keychain when an app is deleted, so the first launch of a new
install signs out a session an older install left.

Only the account host ends a session. When the relay turns the session down at its
socket (`signed_out`, or a 401 to the upgrade), the app asks `GET /me`: a 401 there
signs out; if the account still accepts the session, the app keeps it, goes back to
the list and says the computer could not be reached with this sign-in, without
trying again by itself. A failed upgrade of any other kind (a 500 from the relay) is
tried again with the usual backoff.

When the relay says a limit is reached, the banner says so once and the app waits:
until midnight UTC for the day's limit, otherwise two minutes, doubling to half an
hour. **Try again** asks at once.

Every iPhone is listed as "iPhone" under signed-in devices: since iOS 16 an app
reads the name a person gave the phone only with an entitlement from Apple.

### Before a release: check the socket's header on a simulator

The session reaches the relay in the `Authorization` header of the WebSocket
upgrade. Apple calls that header reserved, and CI only compiles the app, so run it
once against a real relay before every release that touches `RelayLink.swift` or
`Account.swift`:

```sh
cd ios && xcodegen generate && open Arakun.xcodeproj   # then Run on an iPhone simulator
```

Sign in, pick a computer that is online, and see the inbox load. If the app goes
back to the list with "could not be reached with this sign-in", the header did not
reach the relay: do not release.

A relay pairing link from before sign-in (`https://<label>.arakun.dev/#p=…`) no
longer works; the app says "Relay links are no longer used. Sign in to reach your
computers.", and pairing credentials kept by an older version are deleted at the
first launch. The sign-in screen then says why, and that Tailscale or the same Wi-Fi
need no account, until a sign-in works.

**Pair on this network** pairs the browser, not this app. On the same network or
over Tailscale, the code in Arakun's Phone tab opens the computer's own page in the
browser, and that page pairs. This app reaches your computers only when you sign in.
A pairing link opened from another app asks before it opens anything.

## What it does

- The inbox: every session, the ones waiting for you first, grouped by project.
- A session, live: messages, tool calls with their output, plans, thinking.
- The photos in your prompts and the images the agent shows. Tap one to see it
  full screen, zoom in and share it.
- Send a prompt (queued while the agent works), Stop, allow or deny a permission,
  answer the agent's questions, load older entries.
- Start a session in any place the computer offers, with the agent, model and
  reasoning you pick. The models and reasoning on offer are the ones the computer
  says that agent has there.
- Change a session's model and reasoning (the slider button over it), from its
  next message on.
- The context use and tokens of a session, over its first entry: a bar turns amber
  at 70% of the context window and red at 90%.
- **Plan limits**, in the menu over the inbox: how much of each window of Claude
  Code's and Droid's plans is used and when it resets, as the computer reads them,
  read again every minute while it is open.
- The files a session changed, turn by turn, and each diff.
- Attach photos to a prompt or a new session, from the library or the camera.
  Each is sent at 1568 pixels on its longest side, as JPEG; up to 10 a prompt.


## Notifications

Settings has a **Notifications** switch, and once it is on, **Turn finished**,
**Needs permission or answer** and **Something went wrong**, as on the phone page.
Each computer keeps which of the three it sends; the switches show the choice of the
computer open now.

The computer sends notices as it does to the phone page: Web Push, encrypted end to
end (RFC 8291). The app makes its own P-256 key and auth secret once and keeps them
in the Keychain, on this device only, in the group `<team>.com.arakun.ios.shared`
that it shares with the notification service extension. Whenever it connects to a
computer while notifications are on, it sends `POST /push` with the subscription,
whose endpoint is the cloud Worker: `<account host>/push/v1/apns/<hex token>`, or
`apns-sandbox` for a Debug build. The Worker passes the sealed notice to APNs unread,
in the `e` of an alert that says "Open Arakun to see what changed.", and the
extension (`Notifications/`) decrypts it and shows the computer's own title and body,
one thread per session. When it cannot, the alert shows as it came.

A tap opens the session, on the computer open now or the one the app would open at
launch; a notice does not say which computer sent it. A notice about the session on
screen does not show. Turning the switch off tells the computer open now. It and
signing out unregister from APNs, so the other computers hear the token is gone the
next time they send. Signing out also deletes the keys.

Push needs the Push Notifications capability on `com.arakun.ios` and the
extension's `com.arakun.ios.notifications`, and the Keychain group on both
(`project.yml` sets the entitlements; automatic signing registers them).

## Crash reports

The app asks once, on first launch, whether to send crash reports, and Settings
changes the answer. Only after a yes does `Arakun/Telemetry.swift` start PostHog's SDK
(pinned in `project.yml`), with its exception autocapture and nothing else: no
screens, taps, sessions or person profile, and no location. A report keeps only
the crash, its stack trace and the app, system and device model, and its message
loses links, paths, emails and tokens (`ArakunKit/Sources/ArakunKit/CrashReport.swift`).
Once the user says no, queued reports wait unsent. Reports go to the desktop's
PostHog project as `$exception` events. Debug builds send nothing.

The SDK installs its crash handlers only when the project's "Enable exception
autocapture" setting in PostHog is on. Stack traces are unsymbolicated until the
build uploads its dSYMs to PostHog. The App Store privacy label must list Crash Data,
not linked to the user and not used for tracking.
